Scientific Framework

The Biopsychosocial Model in University Well-Being

In compliance with SDG 3 benchmarks, student health cannot be isolated to singular symptoms. George Engel’s Biopsychosocial Model reveals how biological biology, psychological perception, and social environments continuously co-create human wellness.

Pillar I Physiology & Neurochemistry

Biological Factors

University academic workloads frequently disrupt the suprachiasmatic nucleus (circadian rhythm) via irregular sleep patterns and chronic nocturnal blue-light exposure.

HPA-Axis & Cortisol Cascade

Sustained high cortisol impairs hippocampal neurogenesis, directly degrading memory consolidation before exams.

Sleep Architecture Deficits

Loss of slow-wave and REM sleep prevents neurotoxic waste clearance via the glymphatic system.

BDNF & Aerobic Exercise

150 mins/week moderate activity elevates brain-derived neurotrophic factor, boosting cognitive endurance.

Biochemical Resilience
Pillar II Cognition & Affect

Psychological Factors

Cognitive appraisals dictate whether academic milestones are experienced as exciting challenges or existential threats to self-worth and future security.

CBT Cognitive Distortions

"All-or-nothing" thinking and catastrophizing exaggerate perceived risk of single assessment outcomes.

ACT Psychological Flexibility

Acceptance of uncomfortable sensations without avoidance enables sustained commitment to core personal values.

Academic Imposter Phenomenon

Affects up to 60% of university entrants, fueling compensatory perfectionism and accelerated burnout.

Cognitive Reframing
Pillar III Community & Ecology

Social & Environmental

Campus environment, peer connectedness, mentorship accessibility, and digital culture strongly moderate individual psychological and biological health.

Campus Belonging as a Buffer

Strong collegiate social networks provide perceived social safety, buffering physiological stress markers.

Digital Comparison Trap

Constant algorithmic social comparison elevates social anxiety and reduces authentic interpersonal connection.

Institutional Support Access

De-stigmatized university counseling centers and peer mentoring catalyze early intervention.

Ecosystem Safety
